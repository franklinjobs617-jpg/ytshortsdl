Planning a Renovation? Here's What Smart Homeowners Are Doing Differently in 2026.

Renovation costs have shifted. So have financing rates. We break down the numbers so you can make a decision with confidence — not guesswork.

MH
Modern Homestead Editorial Updated June 4, 2026 · 6 min read
✓ Fact-Checked

If you've priced a kitchen remodel or a bathroom update lately, you already know: renovation costs are not what they were three years ago. Material prices have stabilized from their pandemic peaks, but labor remains tight in most markets — and that means quotes come in higher than most people expect.

That reality has changed how homeowners pay for renovations. The days of automatically reaching for a HELOC are fading. More homeowners are comparing multiple financing paths — and some are landing on options they hadn't considered before.

The Three Ways Most People Finance a Renovation Right Now

Broadly, renovation financing falls into three buckets. Each has tradeoffs — and the "best" one depends almost entirely on your timeline, your equity, and how much you need.

1. Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC)

The traditional choice. You borrow against your home's value, typically at a variable rate. Rates have been running between 7.5% and 9.5% for qualified borrowers in 2026. The application process takes 2–6 weeks, requires an appraisal, and comes with closing costs that can range from 2% to 5% of the loan amount.

2. Cash-Out Refinance

You refinance your entire mortgage for more than you currently owe and take the difference in cash. This made sense when mortgage rates were under 4%. At current rates in the mid-6% range, it's a harder equation to make work — especially if you locked in a low rate during 2020–2021.

3. Unsecured Personal Loan

No home equity required. No appraisal. No closing costs. Fixed rates typically range from 6.99% to 25.99% depending on your credit profile. Funding can arrive as soon as the same day after approval. This option has grown significantly in popularity for mid-sized renovations — roughly $25,000 to $75,000 — where the speed and simplicity outweigh the potentially higher rate.

"For a $40,000 kitchen remodel, I closed my HELOC application after four weeks of paperwork and went with a fixed-rate loan instead. Funded in two days. No lien on the house. The rate difference was less than 1%."
— Homeowner, Austin TX

What the Numbers Actually Look Like

Let's put real figures on the table. On a $40,000 renovation financed over 5 years:

  • HELOC at 8.5% variable — roughly $821/month, plus $800–$2,000 in closing costs. Rate can shift quarterly.
  • Personal loan at 9.99% fixed — roughly $849/month. No closing costs. No lien. Funds in 1–3 days.
  • Credit cards at 22% — nearly $1,100/month minimum, and that assumes you stop using the card entirely.

The monthly difference between the HELOC and the personal loan? About $28. For many homeowners, that's a reasonable premium to pay for speed, certainty, and not putting a second lien on the property.
